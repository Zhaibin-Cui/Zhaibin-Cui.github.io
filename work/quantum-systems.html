01 / PHYSICAL PLATFORM

Build the experiment around its constraints.

Optical access, mechanical stability, vacuum interfaces and RF delivery have to be considered together. A change at one layer can affect alignment, heating, calibration and the quality of the final quantum state.

What had to work

The practical task was to bring heterogeneous components into a reproducible experiment: align the optical paths, establish stable operating points and coordinate state preparation, excitation and detection.

02 / ATOM–PHOTON INTERFACE

Collect the photon and match the fiber mode.

The useful signal is a photon emitted by one atom and coupled into a single-mode fiber. High collection solid angle alone is insufficient: the collected field must also match the fiber mode while preserving the polarization information used for entanglement.

From ray tracing to field overlap

I worked on the objective and collection optics with Zemax, connecting geometrical ray tracing to diffraction-limited focusing and Gaussian-mode matching. The design also had to fit around the chamber, trapping electrodes and addressing beams.

A larger collection cone does not automatically improve fiber coupling. Aberrations and polarization-dependent spatial structure affect the overlap with the fiber mode. I evaluated those constraints together, rather than optimizing numerical aperture in isolation.

03 / INSTRUMENT SOFTWARE

One control interface across the laboratory.

Lasers, RF sources, cameras and photon counters expose different control models. I developed the interfaces that make them usable together, from board firmware and device protocols to higher-level calibration procedures.

Hardware, firmware and software

I independently implemented PCB and embedded-control work, device drivers, communication protocols, APIs and user interfaces. The resulting software connects dozens of instruments and exposes repeatable operations for laser control, spectroscopy, acquisition and calibration.

Routine operations include laser-mode checks, PDH lock recovery, phase calibration and parameter sweeps. Device state and operation boundaries are kept explicit so an experiment can be inspected and reproduced.

Reusable procedures and agent-assisted calibrationInstrument APIs, constrained tools and execution records

Reusable experiment procedures

Drivers and APIs expose instrument functions to calibration and acquisition routines. Measurements, fitted parameters and device state remain part of the experimental record.

Agent-assisted calibration

I exposed selected procedures through constrained tools and Skills, with parameter limits and operation logs. These tools assist specific calibration tasks; deterministic feedback remains on the real-time hardware.

04 / BELL CTRL

Convert detection events into phase-correct control.

Photons arrive probabilistically. Their arrival times carry phase information that the control system must use before the next operation. Bell Ctrl connects timestamp acquisition, event selection, phase computation and DDS updates in a synchronized hardware path.

Event selection and system integrationTDC timestamps, phase updates and my contribution

Event-resolved control

A four-channel TDC timestamps the detection events. Temporal-window selection and programmable arithmetic turn accepted events into phase updates, with synchronized clock and trigger distribution across boards.

Contribution and validation

My contribution sits in the collaborative system-development and experimental-integration work. The measured timing and coherence results below characterize the complete team system.

SUPPORT OPTICS / PDH

A stable optical reference
for coherent control.

Laser-frequency drift changes the detuning of coherent operations. The Pound–Drever–Hall (PDH) assembly supplies the optical error signal used for frequency feedback; its alignment and electronic operating point belong to the same calibration problem.

I connected optical alignment and apparatus integration to instrument interfaces, lock monitoring and recovery procedures. The instrument-control case study shows the working panels used to operate and diagnose the reference during an experiment.
