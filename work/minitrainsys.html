MiniTrainSys: controlled pretraining and knowledge readout

I built MiniTrainSys to study how language models learn and access factual knowledge. The system covers document processing, tokenization, model training, distributed execution, checkpointing, probes, and routing analysis. It lets me vary the data or model while keeping the rest of an experiment under control. The central question is why a model can memorize a fact yet fail to retrieve it.

A training stack built for controlled experiments

I implemented document preparation, byte-level BPE, token sharding, padding-free packing, dense and sparse MoE decoders, BF16 training, DDP/FSDP, and resumable distributed checkpoints using DCP. The OpsBackend interface separates the model definition from PyTorch, Triton, and CUDA implementations, so backend comparisons use the same model and configuration.

Indexed document spans make data order a controlled experimental variable. A manifest records tokenizer fingerprints, shard hashes and preparation settings; Configured fingerprint validation rejects tokenizer/corpus mismatches before the first step. Memory-mapped shards and a bounded mapping cache avoid loading the corpus into RAM. Deterministic epoch-level document shuffling changes the logical order without rewriting the shards, while assigning disjoint, equal-length block sequences to distributed ranks.

End-to-end training performance

Performance measurements include data loading, forward and backward passes, optimizer updates, logging, and synchronization. I report matched-batch and matched-memory comparisons separately, supported by numerical parity checks, batch-capacity sweeps, and raw measurements. Measuring the complete training step shows which implementation changes actually reduce the cost of an experiment.

Recovery and measurement are part of the same stack. FSDP checkpoints save distributed model and Adam states through DCP; evaluation loads a consolidated model independently of optimizer state. Resume selects committed checkpoints. Training logs separate language-model cross-entropy from router regularization, aggregate across the logging interval and ranks, and track data wait, gradient norms and expert-load balance alongside throughput. This makes a failed or slow run diagnosable without changing the experiment it was meant to test.

How data presentation affects factual readout

Following the bioS methodology of Allen-Zhu and Li, I generated biographies for 100,000 synthetic people with six facts each and pretrained two matched MoEs for approximately 4B tokens apiece. Both use the same fact table: one sees fixed biographies; the other sees five paraphrases with sentence-order permutation. Both nearly memorize the source text. With frozen-backbone Q probes and only a name as input, first-token readout accuracy is 12.83% and 98.79%, respectively.

Probes and interventions on factual access

With the backbone frozen, I measure first-token and whole-value readout separately using biography-prefix P probes and name-only Q probes. Relation-exposure controls test whether later placement alone explains a readout failure. Routing difference-in-differences compares continuations that share a first token. A separate intervention supplies the true first token and trains a fresh probe to test conditional access to the complete value.

What the results establish, and what remains open

In these experiments, augmentation makes attribute-entry tokens accessible from a name, while recovery of the complete value remains sensitive to the supplied token and context. This motivates further tests of data that exposes reusable components and intermediate steps. Whether these findings extend to natural corpora or larger models remains open.

In progress · Hidden reasoning

I am using synthetic tasks to vary reasoning depth, dependency structure, and surface form independently, and to study how these factors affect computation in hidden states.

In progress · Regret and error recovery

I am studying error recognition, backtracking, and correction trajectories to test how training on errors and corrections affects reasoning behavior.

In progress · CFG and structural generalization

I am using context-free grammars to control hierarchy and composition and to test generalization to configurations absent from training.

What comes next?

  • Does the model learn a whole template, or knowledge that can be independently accessed and recombined?
  • What causes the gap between first-token and complete-value readout?
  • How do data, routing, and architecture change internal access to knowledge?
Experimental conditions & contribution scope
Performance results apply to the model, software, hardware, and measurement conditions reported in the repository.
The scheduled-token budgets differ by 0.62%. Identities held out from probe training were included in backbone pretraining.
The intervention combines a supplied true first token with a newly trained probe; it does not measure autonomous answer generation. Routing differences do not establish that individual experts store particular facts.
Hidden reasoning, Regret, and CFG generalization are ongoing studies; the completed bioS experiments concern factual readout.
