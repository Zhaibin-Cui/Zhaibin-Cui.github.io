MiniMax: pretraining research at scale

At MiniMax, I participate in large-scale GPU pretraining and research the data and training decisions behind model capability. My work covers data-mixture and document-packing experiments, scaling studies, benchmark development, and implementation within the team’s shared data and training pipelines. I connect controlled comparisons to training dynamics and capability evaluation, so a promising recipe can be assessed beyond a single run.

From a training hypothesis to a scale decision

An experiment begins with a decision it needs to inform: which data or training change merits a larger run, and what comparison would justify that choice? I translate the hypothesis into a controlled setup, contribute the required data or training-input changes, and carry the comparison through implementation and evaluation within the team’s existing stack.

The practical bottleneck is experiment selection: which change is worth a larger training run? I compare convergence behavior and downstream capability, examine whether recipe rankings persist across scales, and account for training stage and compute budget. These comparisons help distinguish a durable improvement from an early learning advantage or an evaluation mismatch.

Data mixtures, exposure and sequence construction

A corpus recipe determines which concepts, domains and forms of reasoning the model repeatedly encounters. My data-strategy work examines source mixtures, quality strata, coverage and document packing as coupled choices. Changing sampling or packing changes the sequences used for optimization, even when the underlying document collection is unchanged.

I contribute pipeline changes needed to run these comparisons, then evaluate the resulting training behavior and capability profile. The aim is to identify useful training distributions under a finite budget, rather than maximize a filter score or optimize token utilization without checking what the model learns.

Testing whether a recipe transfers across scale

I use multi-scale training experiments and scaling-law analysis to study the interaction of model capacity, data and compute. Smaller experiments provide a way to screen hypotheses; held-out comparisons at other scales test whether those findings are useful for allocating a larger training budget.

I distinguish faster learning at an intermediate checkpoint from better performance at the target budget. Recipe ordering, the shape of the learning trajectory and capability-specific changes matter alongside a fitted trend. This keeps scale extrapolation tied to an experimental decision: extend a run, revise the control, or examine a different part of the evaluation.

Diagnosing capability through the training process

I contribute to benchmark development and evaluation workflows that connect pretraining progress to knowledge and reasoning behavior. Comparing checkpoints under consistent evaluation conditions helps separate a change in the model from a change in the measurement. Capability-specific results reveal trade-offs that an aggregate score can conceal.

Large-scale GPU training also exposes the execution constraints behind a proposed experiment. Memory pressure, communication, data delivery and numerical behavior affect both throughput and the runs that can be completed reliably. I consider these constraints together with convergence and model capability when interpreting a data or training intervention.

Connecting training observations to model structure

My broader research asks how a model’s computational structure governs information access and learning. I study attention and positional representations, sparse expert routing, recurrent and state-space computation, and the role of auxiliary objectives. The useful question is what behavior a design choice predicts, and which controlled comparison could distinguish that explanation from a data effect.

I pursue this mechanistic angle in my independent MiniTrainSys experiments and technical writing. DeepSeek-V3, Olmo 3 and NVIDIA Nemotron provide public reference points for architecture, data and optimization choices. They inform the questions discussed here; their implementations and reported results are not MiniMax contributions of mine.

Experimental conditions & contribution scope
The training infrastructure and data pipelines are team-developed. My contribution is pretraining research, experiments and implementation within those systems.
Figures explain public research concepts. Internal model configurations, data ratios, training curves and evaluation results are not disclosed.

Testing the mechanism behind a training result.

A data change can improve recall without improving flexible access to knowledge. In my independent MiniTrainSys study, matched sparse-MoE runs and frozen-backbone probes separate source-text memorization from factual readout. This gives me a concrete way to investigate the mechanisms that an aggregate training metric leaves unresolved.

That experimental habit informs my industrial research: identify the capability being changed, keep the comparison interpretable, and test whether the explanation remains useful at another scale or training stage. The public notebook connects this approach to data coverage, architectural inductive biases and reasoning.
