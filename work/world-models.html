01 / RESEARCH PERSPECTIVE

A useful model should survive contact with the world.

My interest in world models comes from a practical question: what has a system actually learned if it can predict an observation, but cannot anticipate the consequences of an intervention? In a physical experiment, a model earns its value by helping us choose a control, interpret a measurement and identify why reality departed from the prediction.

I see a related problem in embodied intelligence. A useful representation needs to preserve object relations, motion, contact and the information hidden by partial observation. It should support decisions at the time scale of the task, while remaining compatible with the bandwidth and uncertainty of the real system.

The long-term direction that interests me is highly generalizable robots: systems that reuse physical and semantic knowledge across tasks, recover when an action fails, and adapt without requiring a new collection campaign for every setting.

This page describes my research interests and reading of the field. My completed work is documented in the language-model, vision and experimental-physics project pages.

02 / A RESEARCH LINEAGE

From compact internal models
to embodied foundation models.

These are complementary lines of work, rather than a single sequence of replacements. The enduring question is what to predict, at what level of abstraction, and how the prediction should influence behavior.

2018

Separate perception, memory and control.

Ha and Schmidhuber made the vision–memory–controller decomposition unusually tangible: a compact predictive latent state could support a small controller, including policies trained in an imagined environment. The idea builds on an older history of learned dynamics and model-based control.

Ha & Schmidhuber · World Models ↗
2019 → 2020

Two ways to use learned dynamics.

MuZero learns task-relevant dynamics for Monte Carlo tree search, predicting rewards, values and policies rather than reconstructing every observation. Dreamer learns an actor and value function through latent imagination. Their 2019 preprints became Nature 2020 and ICLR 2020 papers, respectively—distinct routes from prediction to behavior.

Schrittwieser et al. · MuZero ↗ · Dreamer (ICLR 2020) ↗
2023 → 2025

Learn behavior through latent imagination.

DreamerV3 trains a recurrent state-space model and improves an actor–critic using imagined latent trajectories. Its 2023 preprint appeared in Nature in 2025. This route emphasizes useful internal rollouts, without requiring online search for every executed action.

Hafner et al. · Mastering diverse control tasks through world models ↗
2023 → 2025

Transfer semantic and motor knowledge.

RT-2 connects vision–language pretraining to action tokens. Open X-Embodiment studies data and policy transfer across robots. π0 introduces a flow-matching action expert; π0.5 explores heterogeneous co-training and hierarchical task execution. These are policy-learning advances, not automatically explicit predictive world models.

Brohan et al. · RT-2 ↗
2024 → 2025

Scale interactive and latent prediction.

Genie 2 and Genie 3 explore interactive generated environments. V-JEPA 2 learns video representations; its action-conditioned extension, V-JEPA 2-AC, is post-trained with robot interaction data for planning. Pixel generation and latent prediction expose different interfaces to an agent.

Google DeepMind · Genie 3 ↗
2026

Couple visual futures with actions.

DreamZero is a concrete World Action Model example: a video-diffusion backbone jointly predicts future video and actions. I am interested in the alignment this creates between intended motion and anticipated consequences. The cited work is a February 2026 preprint.

03 / REPRESENTATION & DYNAMICS

What is the model being asked to preserve?

A video generator models plausible visual continuations. An action-conditioned world model must additionally represent how a particular intervention changes the future. A VLA policy maps observations and instructions to actions; it may benefit from an internal predictive representation without exposing a separately queryable dynamics model.

I use World Action Model here for approaches that couple future-state prediction and action generation, with DreamZero as a specific example. The term does not identify one universal architecture. I would examine the conditional inputs, prediction targets, temporal alignment and training losses before treating two systems as equivalent.

STATE IS MORE THAN A FRAME

Memory, geometry and hidden variables.

One image rarely determines velocity, friction, occluded objects or the state of a contact. I am interested in representations that integrate observation history and proprioception, preserve task-relevant geometry, and maintain uncertainty over what the sensors cannot establish.

THE TARGET SHAPES THE REPRESENTATION

Pixels, features—or both.

Pixel reconstruction offers dense supervision and an inspectable future, but also spends capacity on appearance. Feature prediction can emphasize more stable structure, while making the quality of the representation and its distance metric central. I would test either choice through downstream decisions and controlled interventions.

For a different predictive target, V-JEPA 2 and V-JEPA 2-AC ↗ separate video representation pretraining from action-conditioned post-training. The distinction matters: learning from unlabelled video alone does not supply the robot action interface.

04 / ACTION, PLANNING & UNCERTAINTY

Prediction becomes useful
when it changes a decision.

I distinguish learning a policy inside a world model from using a world model to search at inference time. The first can amortize planning into a fast policy. The second can compare candidate action sequences, execute a short prefix and replan from the next observation. Their compute and latency costs are very different.

In either case, the model should rank feasible futures correctly. A visually convincing rollout is insufficient if it ignores action magnitude, loses object identity, or invents a successful contact. I would inspect counterfactual sensitivity, accumulated state error and the relation between model error and closed-loop task performance.

zt+1∼pθ(zt+1∣zt,at)

A compact notation for action-conditioned dynamics. Here, z summarizes relevant history; it is not assumed to be the complete physical state. A predictive distribution must be evaluated for calibration, not merely sampled to produce a plausible continuation.

Uncertainty should affect the action.

Ambiguity from limited observations differs from uncertainty caused by missing training coverage. I am interested in whether a model can expose these limitations well enough to shorten the planning horizon, request another observation or select a more informative action. This requires calibration and intervention studies, not confidence-shaped graphics.

Control has a wall clock.

Action chunk length, sensing delay, inference time and actuator frequency jointly determine the effective feedback rate. Longer chunks can improve throughput while postponing correction. An embodied model needs evaluation under the timing and compute budget of its deployment, including asynchronous execution and stale observations.

Further comparisons: Genie 2 ↗ / Genie 3 ↗ explore interactive visual environments; DreamZero ↗ connects joint prediction to a real-time policy interface. Attractive generated scenes alone do not establish control accuracy.

05 / DATA & ROBOT GENERALIZATION

What transfers—and what must be relearned?

Web video provides broad coverage of motion and scene changes; robot trajectories attach actions to outcomes. Language supplies goals and semantic structure. Their supervision is complementary. I would study the data mixture by what it contributes—object understanding, contact-rich transitions, failure recovery or long-horizon composition—rather than by volume alone.

Cross-embodiment learning also needs explicit interfaces. Joint coordinates, end-effector deltas, gripper commands and control frequencies are not interchangeable. Camera calibration, timestamps, units, coordinate frames and action conventions can determine whether a seemingly shared behavior is actually consistent across datasets.

π0.5 ↗ is relevant to this question because it co-trains on heterogeneous robot and multimodal examples, combining high-level semantic steps with low-level actions. π0 ↗ illustrates a different part of the interface: a vision–language backbone coupled to continuous action generation through flow matching.

Generalization needs named axes.

New lighting, unseen objects, new task compositions and a different robot are distinct shifts. I would hold them out separately before combining them. A model should be tested on recoveries and failures as well as successful demonstrations, with clear accounting of adaptation data and environment-specific tuning.

Evaluation should expose the bottleneck.

A useful report separates predictive fidelity, action selection and physical execution. I would track task completion, intervention rate, recovery behavior, latency and data efficiency, alongside repeated trials and uncertainty estimates. This helps distinguish a representation failure from an interface or control failure.

06 / QUESTIONS I WANT TO PURSUE

Mechanisms first.
Experiments close behind.

01 / DATA

Which experiences teach reusable dynamics?

Compare mixtures by transition diversity, contact events, failure coverage and temporal structure. Keep compute, evaluation and contamination controls fixed when testing whether an apparent gain transfers across scales.

02 / REPRESENTATION

Which information survives compression?

Probe whether latent states preserve geometry, velocity, object persistence and uncertainty. Test those properties under intervention, rather than relying on a representation’s visual appearance or a single downstream score.

03 / REASONING

When does imagining help?

Compare direct policies, model-based search and hybrid approaches under the same latency budget. Study the horizon at which imagined outcomes become unreliable and whether a model can recognize that boundary.

04 / ARCHITECTURE

How should prediction and action share structure?

Examine shared versus specialized dynamics and action modules, temporal tokenization, state memory and embodiment conditioning. Measure effects on transfer and closed-loop control as well as training loss.

The experimental habits I bring.

Quantum experiments taught me to connect a theoretical model to an optical path, an electronic control sequence and a measurable result. Model training and vision research add a complementary habit: build the smallest useful implementation, instrument it, and use controlled comparisons to decide what to change. I want to bring that combination to predictive models and embodied intelligence.
