PaGE: spatially aligned gaze estimation and model distillation

PaGE estimates a person’s gaze target by combining scene semantics with local head cues. It aligns and exchanges features between two visual streams, then distills a large teacher model into smaller students. I contributed to scene–head interaction, spatial positional encoding, distillation-data curation, and distillation experiments.

Scene–head interaction in shared spatial coordinates

DINOv3 encodes the scene and head crop separately. The Scene-head Interaction Module (SIM) uses bidirectional cross-attention, followed by branch-specific self-attention and GEGLU MLPs. Mapping head patches into scene coordinates lets 2D RoPE encode their spatial relationship to scene patches in a shared coordinate system. The model predicts a gaze heatmap and the probability that the target lies inside the image.

Distillation data without manual gaze labels

I contributed to curating approximately 608,000 images and 1.17 million head regions from MPII and OpenImages V7. YOLOv5 head detection supplies crops without manual gaze labels. COCO sources are excluded to avoid overlap with the evaluation data. Teacher-generated representations on these images provide a larger training set for distillation than the gaze-annotated data alone.

Transferring teacher representations to smaller models

A PaGE ViT-H+ teacher supervises token-level L1 and cosine feature alignment in both streams, with an auxiliary constraint on backbone features. Supervised fine-tuning follows distillation. Ablations examine register tokens, GEGLU, 2D RoPE, and distillation objectives to assess the contribution of these design choices.

Accuracy, inference cost, and interactive use

The distilled ViT-B exceeds the paper’s human baselines on seven of nine metrics across GazeFollow, VAT, and ChildPlay. ViT-S reduces inference cost while retaining strong performance; the paper reports BF16 profiling on an RTX 4090 and an i9-14900K. Public checkpoints, inference scripts, and a demo support use in interactive prototypes.

Qualitative generalization and failure cases

The paper presents qualitative examples involving animals, cameras, and animated characters, as well as screen-gaze experiments, annotation errors, and failure cases. These examples help identify questions about scene semantics, spatial precision, dataset coverage, and evaluation reliability.

What comes next?

  • What interaction structure makes global semantics and local cues complement each other?
  • Can diverse data without gaze labels transfer large-model capability to a small model?
  • As models reach human baselines, do data bias and evaluation errors become the next bottlenecks?
Experimental conditions & contribution scope
PaGE is collaborative; paper-level outcomes and individual contributions are identified separately.
Scene and head are two visual streams; this work is not evidence of language–vision or tactile model training.
Examples involving animals, cameras, and animated characters are qualitative; they do not constitute a separate quantitative out-of-distribution benchmark.
Evidence for practical use includes model compression, public inference code, and CPU/GPU profiling. Production product or robot deployments are not reported here.
