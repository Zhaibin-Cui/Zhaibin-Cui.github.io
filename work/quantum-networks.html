Kilometer-scale trapped-ion entanglement and programmable quantum memory

I built two trapped-ion platforms from scratch, integrating theoretical analysis, precision optics, ultra-high vacuum, control electronics and dual-node experiments. I led experimental work on 12 km ion–photon and 1.2 km ion–ion entanglement, and collaborated on programmable photonic quantum memory, long-lived DFS storage and functional dual-type network nodes.

Laser-frequency drift changes the detuning of coherent operations. The Pound–Drever–Hall (PDH) assembly supplies the optical error signal used for frequency feedback; its alignment and electronic operating point belong to the same calibration problem.

I connected optical alignment and apparatus integration to instrument interfaces, lock monitoring and recovery procedures. The instrument-control case study shows the working panels used to operate and diagnose the reference during an experiment.

The problem

Remote entanglement generation is probabilistic. Fiber loss and round-trip heralding delays limit generation rates, while decoherence degrades stored quantum correlations. I study the joint design of multiplexing, encoding, optical interfaces and control timing to match entanglement-generation timescales to memory lifetimes.

My contribution and implementation

  • Designed and integrated ion-trap hardware, laser and RF systems, ultra-high-vacuum assemblies and experimental software. Established procedures for ion loading, cooling, state preparation, control and tomography, and evaluated system performance through error and stability measurements.
  • Designed the optical interface for coupling atomic single-photon emission into single-mode fiber, combining high-NA objectives, aberration control, diffraction modeling and Gaussian-mode matching. Evaluated how collection efficiency and polarization preservation affect entanglement fidelity and link-generation rate.
  • Led hybrid-multiplexing experiments with up to 44 time bins and roughly 16-fold enhancement of the entanglement-generation rate over 12 km. Extended temporal multiplexing to two remote trapped-ion nodes, achieving a 4.59-fold speedup with ten time bins.
  • Collaborated on programmable photonic quantum memory, dual-type communication and memory qubits, DFS encoding and long-lived entanglement storage. The work combines quantum information theory, quantum-algorithm foundations, numerical modeling and experimental validation.

Photon indistinguishability at the network interface

Efficient photon collection and high-fidelity remote entanglement impose different requirements. Photons arriving at the Bell-state analyzer must overlap in spatial mode, arrival time, complex spectral-temporal mode and polarization, so that their origin is not distinguishable. My work on collection optics, single-mode-fiber coupling, polarization alignment and synchronized control sits at this interface. The network result depends on these layers operating together, not on collection efficiency alone.

Distributed orchestration and deterministic execution

The two-node control design in my research presentation distinguishes Alice and Bob management computers from the master/slave sequencers, AWG and entanglement-control hardware. Host-side coordination connects instrument configuration and experiment orchestration; the synchronized electronic path handles time-critical state operations and event-dependent feedback. My work connects device interfaces, calibration procedures and experimental integration across this separation.

Metropolitan ion–photon link

Nature Communications 17, 697: 44 time bins, a heralded entanglement-generation rate of 4.28 s⁻¹ over 12 km and a memory coherence time of 366 ms. Hybrid multiplexing increases the rate by about 16-fold.

High-fidelity remote ion entanglement

PRL 137, 020803: 1.2 km, ten time bins, a 4.59-fold speedup and 95.9 ± 1.5% fidelity. The result set a fidelity record at the time for heralded matter–matter entanglement over extended fiber distances and was featured by Phys.org.

Programmable quantum memory

PRX 14, 021018: storage of 72 optical qubits with over a thousand consecutive random-access write/read operations, demonstrating quantum queues, stacks and buffers.

DFS and functional network nodes

Coauthored studies investigate crosstalk-free dual-type nodes, local entangling gates and decoherence-free-subspace (DFS) memories. The PRL memory study reports a fitted coherence time above two hours after leakage postselection. In the PRA entangled-memory study, measured storage times extend to 960 s; exponential fits give lifetime bounds on the order of an hour. These conditioned and fitted results are distinguished from directly measured storage durations in the paper atlas.
