01 / APPARATUS

Start with an experiment that can stay running.

I built two ion-trap platforms from scratch, working across the trap, ultra-high-vacuum assembly, lasers, radio-frequency electronics and experiment software. The purpose of that integration is concrete: load and cool ions reliably, prepare the intended state, repeat the sequence and know which part of the system limits the result.

That work requires moving between physical models and practical constraints. Vacuum compatibility, mechanical access, optical alignment, electrical noise and timing are coupled design choices. I treat calibration and error diagnosis as part of the apparatus, so a change in a measured fidelity can be traced back to the experimental sequence and its hardware.

02 / ATOM–PHOTON INTERFACE

Turn a single emitted photon into a usable link.

The optical interface connects an atomic dipole emitter to a single spatial mode of fiber. High numerical aperture increases collection, but coupling also depends on aberrations, diffraction, Gaussian mode matching and polarization. My optical work combines objective design and alignment with propagation analysis to make those constraints compatible.

The system is evaluated through the quantum link it supports: photon collection, transmission, state fidelity and entanglement-generation rate. Frequency conversion extends the interface to telecom fiber; communication–memory encoding protects stored information while the communication ion keeps emitting photons.

03 / CONTROL

Make stochastic events produce reproducible actions.

I independently developed an instrument-control stack from PCB design and embedded firmware through drivers, a unified protocol, APIs and experiment interfaces. It brings dozens of heterogeneous instruments into reusable procedures for scans, acquisition, locking and calibration.

In the collaborative Bell Ctrl project, I contributed to system development and experimental integration of FPGA/RISC-V control. Photon timestamps drive event selection, phase calculation and DDS updates. The reported system reaches 11.5 ps RMS timing precision and approximately 800 ns end-to-end feed-forward latency, validated with hardware measurements and restored Ramsey oscillations.

These are distinct layers: device APIs organize the experiment, while deterministic hardware executes the time-critical sequence. The feed-forward manuscript is a separate electronics collaboration, linked below; its status remains submitted.

04 / MEASURED RESULTS

Judge the system by the quantum information it preserves.

My lead experiments address the rate–fidelity–lifetime trade-off of a network link. The 12 km ion–photon experiment uses hybrid multiplexing to make entanglement generation faster than memory decoherence. The 1.2 km ion–ion experiment extends temporal multiplexing to remote matter qubits, reaching 95.9 ± 1.5% entanglement fidelity.

The latter set a fidelity record at the time for heralded matter-node entanglement over extended fiber distances and was covered by Phys.org. Alongside these lead projects, my coauthored work investigates programmable photonic storage, protected logical qubits and functional dual-type network nodes.

01 / REMOTE MATTER–MATTER ENTANGLEMENT

Temporally Multimode Ion-Ion Entanglement over 1.2 Kilometer Fibers

Z.-B. Cui, Z.-Q. Wang, P.-Y. Liu, Y. Wang, P.-C. Lai, J.-X. Shi, Y.-D. Sun, Z.-C. Tian, H.-S. Sun, Y.-B. Liang, B.-X. Qi, Y.-Y. Huang, Z.-C. Zhou, Y.-K. Wu, Y. Xu, Y.-F. Pu, L.-M. Duan

Physical Review Letters 137, 020803 (2026)Published

Co-first author · led experimental work on the remote-ion system

Research problem

How can two remote ions attempt entanglement more often without sacrificing the fidelity of a successful event?

Experimental approach

Temporal multiplexing places several photon-emission attempts inside one heralding window. Dual-type encoding separates communication and memory, while event-resolved feed-forward compensates the phase associated with photon arrival times.

What the study establishes

Ten temporal modes yield a 4.59-fold rate enhancement over 1.2 km of fiber, with a remote ion–ion entanglement fidelity of 95.9 ± 1.5%.

02 / METROPOLITAN PHOTONIC INTERCONNECT

Metropolitan-scale ion-photon entanglement via a quantum network node with hybrid multiplexing enhancements

Z.-B. Cui, Z.-Q. Wang, P.-C. Lai, Y. Wang, J.-X. Shi, P.-Y. Liu, Y.-D. Sun, Z.-C. Tian, Y.-B. Liang, B.-X. Qi, Y.-Y. Huang, Z.-C. Zhou, Y.-K. Wu, Y. Xu, L.-M. Duan, Y.-F. Pu

Nature Communications 17, 697 (2026)Published

First author · led the hybrid-multiplexing experiment

Research problem

Can a heralded photonic link generate entanglement faster than a node loses its stored quantum coherence?

Experimental approach

Hybrid multiplexing combines repeated excitation within the heralding window with communication–memory encoding. Optical transmission, state manipulation and event timing are designed together around the round-trip delay of the link.

What the study establishes

The experiment realizes ion–photon entanglement over 12 km of fiber using up to 44 time bins. A 4.28 s⁻¹ heralded rate and 366 ms memory coherence time put the average entanglement-generation time below the memory coherence time.

03 / COMMUNICATION WITHOUT ERASING MEMORY

Realization of a Crosstalk-Free Two-Ion Node for Long-Distance Quantum Networking

P.-C. Lai, Y. Wang, J.-X. Shi, Z.-B. Cui, Z.-Q. Wang, S. Zhang, P.-Y. Liu, Z.-C. Tian, Y.-D. Sun, X.-Y. Chang, B.-X. Qi, Y.-Y. Huang, Z.-C. Zhou, Y.-K. Wu, Y. Xu, Y.-F. Pu, L.-M. Duan

Physical Review Letters 134, 070801 (2025)Published

Coauthor · collaborative trapped-ion network research

Research problem

How can one ion keep quantum information while a neighboring ion is repeatedly cooled, excited and measured?

Experimental approach

The communication and memory qubits occupy spectrally separated subspaces of the same calcium-ion species. Raman and quadrupole transitions connect the encodings; frequency conversion brings emitted photons into the telecom band.

What the study establishes

A functioning two-ion node combines repetitive communication operations with protected memory. The photonic interface is characterized over fiber lengths extending to 12 km.

04 / FROM A NODE TO A QUANTUM PROTOCOL

Realization of a functioning dual-type trapped-ion quantum network node

Yuan-Yuan Huang, Lu Feng, Yu-Kai Wu, Yu-Lin Xu, Lin Zhang, Zhai-Bin Cui, Chuan-Xin Huang, Chi Zhang, Shi-An Guo, Quan-Xin Mei, Bin-Xiang Qi, Yong Xu, Yun-Fei Pu, Zi-Chao Zhou, Lu-Ming Duan

Science Advances 11, eaeb4076 (2025)Published

Coauthor · collaborative dual-type-node research

Research problem

Can communication, memory and a local entangling gate operate together as a useful quantum-network node?

Experimental approach

Two ytterbium ions switch between communication and memory encodings. Local entangling gates connect the stored state to the photonic interface, enabling state teleportation and a three-qubit GHZ-state demonstration.

What the study establishes

The measured mean teleportation fidelity is 87.2 ± 0.9%, with a reconstructed process fidelity of 80.7 ± 0.6%. The teleportation demonstration uses classical postprocessing after photonic measurement; it is not an active real-time feed-forward demonstration.

05 / QUANTUM DATA STRUCTURES

Realization of a Programmable Multipurpose Photonic Quantum Memory with Over-Thousand Qubit Manipulations

Sheng Zhang, Jixuan Shi, Zhaibin Cui, Ye Wang, Yukai Wu, Luming Duan, Yunfei Pu

Physical Review X 14, 021018 (2024)Published

Coauthor · programmable photonic-memory collaboration

Research problem

Can a quantum memory do more than store and retrieve one state at a predetermined time?

Experimental approach

Encoding converters and independently addressed atomic ensembles provide a random-access interface for photonic qubits. Programmable scheduling turns that physical memory into queues, stacks and buffers, with experimentally measured output states.

What the study establishes

A 72-qubit memory supports over a thousand sequential write/read operations. Demonstrations include FIFO and LIFO access, buffering and the storage and reordered retrieval of entangled photons.

06 / A LONG-LIVED LOGICAL QUBIT

Long-Time Storage of a Qubit Encoded in Decoherence-Free Subspace Using a Dual-Type Quantum Memory

Y.-L. Xu, L. Zhang, C. Zhang, Y.-K. Wu, Y.-Y. Chen, C.-X. Huang, Z.-B. Cui, R. Yao, W.-Q. Lian, J.-Y. Ma, W.-X. Guo, B.-X. Qi, P.-Y. Hou, Y.-F. Pu, Z.-C. Zhou, L. He, L.-M. Duan

Physical Review Letters 135, 230803 (2025)Published

Coauthor · collaborative DFS-memory research

Research problem

How can a stored qubit reject correlated dephasing while making leakage visible in the readout?

Experimental approach

A logical qubit is encoded across two memory ions in a decoherence-free subspace. Dual-type encoding permits sympathetic cooling, and multistate detection separates leakage events from the intended qubit subspace.

What the study establishes

The paper reports a fitted coherence time exceeding two hours after postselecting leakage-free outcomes. The figure retains both raw and postselected data so the conditioning is explicit.

07 / PRESERVING ENTANGLED LOGICAL STATES

Storage of entangled logical states in decoherence-free subspaces for 10³ seconds

L. Zhang, Y.-L. Xu, Y.-K. Wu, C. Zhang, Z.-B. Cui, Y.-Y. Chen, W.-Q. Lian, J.-Y. Ma, B.-X. Qi, Y.-F. Pu, Z.-C. Zhou, L. He, P.-Y. Hou, L.-M. Duan

Physical Review A 114, 012448 (2026)Published

Coauthor · collaborative entangled-memory research

Research problem

Does the protection of a decoherence-free subspace extend from one logical qubit to entanglement between logical qubits?

Experimental approach

Four memory ions encode two logical qubits, with edge ions providing sympathetic cooling. Native gates prepare logical Bell states; type conversion, spin echo and multistate detection support long storage and leakage-resolved analysis.

What the study establishes

Logical-state fidelities are measured at storage times up to 960 s after discarding leakage events. Exponential fits give lower 68% confidence bounds of 3.2 × 10³ s and 4.9 × 10³ s for the two state lifetimes; these are fitted lifetime bounds, not direct hour-long data points.

RESEARCH PRACTICE

A systems view, grounded in controlled experiments.

Quantum experiments trained me to connect a mathematical description to a physical implementation, isolate failure mechanisms and distinguish an attractive explanation from a measured effect. That same discipline informs my work on model representations, data and learning systems: make the hypothesis precise, build a test that can falsify it, and understand the result well enough to improve the next experiment.
