01 / RESEARCH STATEMENT

How models learn and use knowledge.

I study how language models acquire knowledge from data and use it to answer questions and reason. My focus is on data presentation, representations, reasoning, and architecture. I use synthetic corpora, controlled pretraining, and frozen-backbone probes to test what a model memorizes, what information can be read out, and how that information is used. I built MiniTrainSys from scratch to implement experiments quickly, reproduce comparisons, and test the limits of their conclusions.

Experimental physics trained me to control variables, analyze errors, and reproduce measurements. I design experiments that distinguish competing explanations, implement the tools they require, and trace unexpected results through the data, training configuration, and model states.

01 / DATA

Data presentation and knowledge acquisition

When the facts are held fixed, how do wording, sentence order, and sampling affect factual readout? How do these effects differ from memorization of the training text?

I vary the presentation of a fixed fact table while holding the model and optimization settings constant. I measure source-text memorization, first-token readout, and whole-value readout separately, and report differences in training-token budgets.

Evidence & next questions +
Completed experiment

In the bioS experiment, I constructed biographies for 100,000 synthetic people with six facts each, using either fixed templates or paraphrased, reordered text. Both models nearly memorized the source text. Frozen-backbone Q probes given only a name achieved first-token readout accuracies of 12.83% and 98.79%, respectively.

Two matched MoEs are trained for approximately 4B tokens each; scheduled-token budgets differ by 0.62%. The result measures frozen-backbone Q-probe readout, not free-form question-answer generation.

Questions to test next

Next, I want to isolate the effects of paraphrasing, sentence order, and sampling, then test whether the readout differences recur with natural corpora, other training recipes, and larger models.

02 / REPRESENTATIONS

Knowledge representations and conditional readout

After a model memorizes a fact, why can the hidden state for a name support first-token readout but fail to recover the complete value? What changes when a token is supplied or the context changes?

I freeze the backbone and compare P probes given a biography prefix with Q probes given only a name. I measure first-token and whole-value readout separately, then supply the true first token and train fresh probes. Routing difference-in-differences provides a separate test of how expert selection changes with the continuation.

Evidence & next questions +
Completed intervention

For the augmented-data model, whole-value readout increased from 45.28% to 96.38% when the true first token was supplied and a fresh probe was trained. This result concerns conditional readout under that joint intervention; it does not isolate the effect of the supplied token alone.

The intervention supplies the first token of the true answer; it does not test autonomous generation of the complete answer. Identities held out from probe training were included in backbone pretraining.

Routing differences characterize the access process; they do not establish that an individual expert stores a particular fact.

Questions to test next

I want to separate three questions: whether the representation contains the information, which inputs make it readable, and how much computation the readout itself performs. Further input and representation interventions will test how these explanations depend on the layer, routing, and architecture.

Related project ↗
03 / REASONING

Hidden reasoning and error correction

How does a model carry out multistep computation without an explicit chain of thought? How do error recognition, backtracking, and correction in training affect the way it solves a task?

I am designing synthetic tasks with controlled dependencies and reasoning depth, comparing correct trajectories with trajectories containing errors and corrections. The design evaluates final answers, intermediate behavior, and hidden states separately.

Evidence & next questions +
Existing experimental foundation

MiniTrainSys provides synthetic-data preparation, dense/MoE pretraining, checkpoint recovery, frozen-backbone probes, and evaluation. The completed mechanism experiments study factual readout; these tools also support the ongoing reasoning studies.

Research in progress

I am investigating hidden reasoning and Regret-style error recovery, drawing on tasks from Physics of Language Models. These studies are in progress; the bioS readout results do not establish conclusions about them.

Related project ↗
04 / ARCHITECTURE

Model architecture and structural generalization

With the same training signal, how do attention, positional encoding, and routing affect a model’s ability to combine what it has learned and handle unseen structures?

For ongoing language experiments, I use context-free grammars (CFGs) to control hierarchy, depth, and composition and to define held-out configurations. In PaGE, ablations of feature interaction, positional encoding, and distillation objectives examine the effects of specific design choices.

Evidence & next questions +
Existing architecture work

In PaGE, I contributed to bidirectional scene–head cross-attention, 2D RoPE in a shared scene coordinate system, and distillation data. The paper examines these designs through architecture and objective ablations. The complete distilled ViT-B exceeds the paper’s human baselines on seven of nine metrics across three datasets.

The seven-of-nine result belongs to the complete model and cannot be attributed to a single module. Both the scene and head branches are visual.

Research in progress

Work on CFG structure learning and generalization is ongoing. With training budgets and model capacity specified, I want to test whether models learn compositional structure and which architectural choices change their performance on unseen structures.

HOW I WORK

How I run experiments.

When two models behave differently, I list plausible explanations and design a minimal controlled experiment to distinguish them. Synthetic data fixes the facts and dependencies; I record training budgets and optimization settings. Frozen-backbone probes, input interventions, and ablations test specific hypotheses about memorization, readout, and computation. I build the data, training, and evaluation tools needed to run these experiments quickly, retaining raw measurements, failure cases, and the conditions behind each conclusion.

TRAINING / RESEARCH

Pretraining across scales.

Compare data recipes and training budgets across model sizes, and test whether results persist at larger scales.

01

From a training hypothesis to a scale decision

An experiment begins with a decision it needs to inform: which data or training change merits a larger run, and what comparison would justify that choice? I translate the hypothesis into a controlled setup, contribute the required data or training-input changes, and carry the comparison through implementation and evaluation within the team’s existing stack.

Methods & measurements ↗
02

Data mixtures, exposure and sequence construction

A corpus recipe determines which concepts, domains and forms of reasoning the model repeatedly encounters. My data-strategy work examines source mixtures, quality strata, coverage and document packing as coupled choices. Changing sampling or packing changes the sequences used for optimization, even when the underlying document collection is unchanged.

Methods & measurements ↗
03

Testing whether a recipe transfers across scale

I use multi-scale training experiments and scaling-law analysis to study the interaction of model capacity, data and compute. Smaller experiments provide a way to screen hypotheses; held-out comparisons at other scales test whether those findings are useful for allocating a larger training budget.

Methods & measurements ↗
04

Diagnosing capability through the training process

I contribute to benchmark development and evaluation workflows that connect pretraining progress to knowledge and reasoning behavior. Comparing checkpoints under consistent evaluation conditions helps separate a change in the model from a change in the measurement. Capability-specific results reveal trade-offs that an aggregate score can conceal.

Methods & measurements ↗
05

Connecting training observations to model structure

My broader research asks how a model’s computational structure governs information access and learning. I study attention and positional representations, sparse expert routing, recurrent and state-space computation, and the role of auxiliary objectives. The useful question is what behavior a design choice predicts, and which controlled comparison could distinguish that explanation from a data effect.

Apparatus & integration ↗
02 / ATOM–PHOTON INTERFACE
One photon.
A carefully matched optical mode.

I worked on high-NA collection and single-mode-fiber coupling, connecting geometrical ray tracing, diffraction-limited propagation and Gaussian-mode overlap. The objective also had to fit around the chamber, trapping electrodes and addressing beams.

Zemax design, aberration control, polarization preservation and alignment all serve the same experimental goal: collect a usable photon without losing the information carried by its state.
